# Fictional-universe fork, merge and cultural dispute

## Situation
Creators build rich Kristals representing separate fictional universes, then fork and combine them to create AI-assisted stories, images or video. Some participants embrace the remix; others reject it aesthetically, culturally or legally.

## Epistemic distinction

\[FictionalRepresentation \neq FactualAssertion\]

and:

\[Fork \neq Endorsement\]

## Knowledge composition

\[Artifact_A + Artifact_B \rightarrow DerivedArtifact_C\]

Preserve provenance, source boundaries, derivative status, semantic conflicts and incompatibilities.

## ethiKos role
Decompose the dispute into separate questions: artistic interest, fidelity, rights, promotion, discoverability, community values, generative reuse, licensing.

## Expertise
Relevant expertise may include writers, actors, artists, literary scholars, lawyers, designers, fandom historians and rights holders.

\[Expertise \neq Sovereignty\]

Artistic expertise does not automatically confer legal authority.

## Smart Vote role
Expose multiple readings such as raw participant signal, domain-expert reading, affected/rightsholder reading and other transparent lenses.

No lens should silently become “the truth.”

## Rights caution
Real commercial universes such as Tolkien or Marvel introduce copyright and trademark constraints. Public-domain or explicitly licensed corpora are safer for demos and publication examples.

## Failure modes
- rights violation;
- expert status becomes aesthetic aristocracy;
- popular fandom overwhelms minority rights;
- default-lens capture;
- provenance erased in derivatives;
- AI work appears endorsed by original creators;
- semantic merge destroys internal consistency.

## Potential tests
- provenance after fork/merge;
- source/derivative comparison;
- license metadata visibility;
- multiple-reading reproducibility;
- preservation of incompatible interpretations;
- deprecation without deleting history.

## What this scenario does not prove
It does not prove rights-holder permission, that expert weighting produces better art, or that structured deliberation resolves cultural conflict.
