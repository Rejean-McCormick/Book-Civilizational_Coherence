# Remote multilingual school connected to global knowledge

## Situation
A remote school accesses structured public knowledge and an open Univers-City-style learning institution in a locally relevant language. Students can participate remotely through Koali when physical attendance is impossible because of illness, severe weather, disability, transport disruption or distance.

Experts formalize knowledge into media, courses, exercises, proofs and evaluated learning paths.

## Coordination problem

\[GlobalKnowledgeAvailable \neq LocalKnowledgeAccessible\]

Losses can arise through language, connectivity, institutional isolation, absence from school, scattered expert knowledge and obsolete educational material.

## Desired transformation

\[ExpertKnowledge \rightarrow StructuredArtifact \rightarrow Media \rightarrow Course \rightarrow Practice \rightarrow PeerReview \rightarrow ReusableMasteryPath\]

and:

\[GlobalKnowledge \rightarrow LocalLanguage \rightarrow LocalLearning\]

## UCKK-model role
Experts can use open production channels to convert expertise into lectures, demonstrations, exercises, course modules, evaluations, portfolios and media-library resources. Peer experts can review content.

Smart Vote-like readings may expose expert agreement/disagreement but should not transform voting into pedagogical truth.

## Koali role

\[PhysicalAbsence \not\Rightarrow EducationalExclusion\]

Remote students can remain connected to classes, projects, peers and structured resources.

## Local sovereignty
The school should retain local curriculum, language, community context, local data and teaching authority, with offline continuity where feasible.

## AI role
AI may accelerate expert documentation, drafting, language-resource construction and testing, while validated deterministic artifacts remain reusable afterward where feasible.

## Real-place caution
If Nain, Labrador is named in the manuscript, independently verify the local language context, institutions, connectivity, accessibility and community preferences. Do not imply endorsement.

## Failure modes
- dominant-language ontology;
- inaccurate translation;
- educational colonialism;
- bandwidth dependency;
- global curriculum displacing local knowledge;
- unusable expert material;
- AI-generated instructional error;
- centralized governance;
- student privacy failure.

## Potential tests
- offline resource coverage;
- semantic preservation;
- remote participation under interruption;
- local/external curriculum control;
- provenance of expert material;
- correction update latency;
- export/reuse of learning artifacts.

## What this scenario does not prove
It does not prove complete Wikidata availability in every indigenous language, adoption by a real school, arbitrary prose support or elimination of educational inequality.
