# Chapter 8 Brief — Power, Capture and Non-Domination

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Credibility and coordination infrastructure create power.

## Proof obligation
Define capture and domination, then derive counterpowers that make capture less terminal rather than pretending it can be eliminated.

## Main mechanism
Control over legitimate functions can become durable cross-domain power. Verification, contestation, substitution and exit reduce dependence.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Power ≠ Formal Authority
- Power ≠ Domination
- Transparency ≠ Accountability
- Formal Exit ≠ Credible Exit
- Technical Forkability ≠ Social Forkability
- Recorded Event ≠ Sanction

## Best worked example
A government captures visibility/credibility rules in a public instance; alternatives fork and try to restore credible service.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
Forkability can fragment; transparency can surveil; anti-capture mechanisms can themselves be captured.

## kOA role
Non-terminal capture, portable knowledge/evidence, contextual sanctions, independent instances. Cultural contestability/narrative precommitment supports recognition, not legal process.

## Epistemic status
Non-domination is normative; non-terminal capture is design aim; credible recovery time is a proposed metric.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Full memory and federation mechanics.

## Exit condition
Power over coordination is partly power over what the system remembers. What makes experience cumulative?
