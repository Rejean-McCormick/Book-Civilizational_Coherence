# Open Good

## Definition

In this project, **open good** names a stronger target than merely publishing source code.

The intended question is not only:

> Can someone inspect or clone the repository?

It is:

> Can someone, where legal rights permit, reproduce a usable functional good and adapt it to their own context without permanent dependence on the original operator?

Conceptually:

\[
OpenSource \subset OpenGoodTarget
\]

This is a **project design principle**, not a universal definition used by all open-source communities.

## Functional layers

An open-good target may include, as rights permit:

- source code;
- schemas and data models;
- configuration;
- deployment instructions;
- operational documentation;
- tests;
- curriculum/templates;
- media or content where licensed for reuse;
- migration/export formats;
- governance procedures;
- interface contracts;
- knowledge needed to operate and modify the system.

## Reproduction test

\[
RepositoryClone \neq FunctionalReproduction
\]

A stronger test is whether an independent person or institution can create a usable derivative.

## UCKK clarification

UCKK itself may remain the creator's own educational, publication and diffusion channel.

The open-good principle does **not** require founder removal.

What is encouraged is independent derivative creation:

\[
UCKK_{original}\rightarrow PatternExtraction\rightarrow IndependentUniversCities
\]

Derivative builders may, where rights permit:

- duplicate the code/model;
- replace King Klown;
- replace the Inquisitor role/name;
- create another training series;
- create a personal channel;
- create an internal corporate academy;
- create a disciplinary or global Univers-City;
- use no mythology at all.

The derivative institution need not be governed by UCKK or its creator.

## Replication and federation

Replication comes before federation.

\[
IndependentInstance \rightarrow OptionalFederation
\]

A derivative should be capable of remaining independent, including local/offline operation where the relevant architecture supports it.

## Non-monetization of the original public good

The project includes a **project-specific normative commitment** that the original UCKK/Koali public-good platform is not intended to be monetized as the public good itself.

This is not presented as a universal theorem about software, institutions, services, labor, hosting, consulting, derivatives or every possible implementation.

Exact legal consequences depend on actual licenses and agreements.

## Legal boundary

Architectural intent does not itself grant legal rights.

\[
ArchitecturallyForkable \neq LegallyReusable
\]

Consult `architecture/RIGHTS_AND_REPLICATION_MATRIX.md` and the source repository's actual license before asserting that code, documentation, content, branding or media may be reused.

## Success signal

A strong open good creates capability outside itself:

\[
Influence \neq Control
\]

\[
OpenGoodSuccess \rightarrow IndependentCapability
\]
