# **Chapter 1 – Fractured Foundations** {#chapter-1-–-fractured-foundations}

## The Breaking Point {#the-breaking-point}

Québec City lay frozen under a sky the color of old steel. Wind howled through the empty streets, sweeping snow in restless spirals beneath flickering streetlights. Inside **Hôtel-Dieu Hospital**, the air was thick, stale—too many people, too much waiting.

Réjean McCormick sat rigid, his hands locked together between his knees. His son lay beside him, slumped in the chair, feverish, shivering. His skin was pale, damp with cold sweat. Each breath came shallow, too fast, then too slow.

**Bacterial meningitis.**

Réjean knew it. He’d spent hours reading, comparing symptoms, double-checking medical sources. He knew what they needed. A single antibiotic. Fast. Before the swelling in his son’s brain became irreversible.

He had told them. Explained. Pleaded.

But all he got was the same tired response.

“I understand your concern, Monsieur McCormick,” the nurse repeated, her tone as sterile as the walls. “But we have procedures. A doctor will see your son soon.”

**Soon.**

That word was an insult. It was a placeholder for **never**.

His son stirred, barely. His eyelids fluttered, unfocused. A weak whimper slipped through cracked lips—a sound that **cut through Réjean like a blade.**

His fists clenched.

The pharmacy sign across the street burned in his peripheral vision, its blue neon buzzing against the night.

**The cure was there.** Behind a counter. Behind a locked door.

His breath came slow. Then steady.

Réjean stood.

He bent down, his lips close to his son’s ear. “I’ll be right back.” A whisper. A promise. A lie.

Outside, the wind sliced into him, but he didn’t feel it. The streets were empty, his path clear. He reached the doors of the pharmacy, pressed his hands against the glass. **It was right there.**

He lifted the rock.

Glass exploded. The sound shattered the quiet. An alarm shrieked into the night. But Réjean was already moving. His hands found the shelf, the vial. **The cure.**

He turned, ran. The cold bit his lungs as he sprinted back, his fingers locked around salvation.

Inside the hospital, the world had already changed.

A doctor stood by his son’s still body, murmuring something to the nurses. Their hands moved with quiet finality, pulling a white sheet over too-small shoulders.

Réjean stopped. His breath caught, his stomach wrenched.

The box of medication slipped from his fingers, hitting the floor with a hollow **clack**.

His son wasn’t there anymore. Only the weight of what had been.

A hand gripped his shoulder.

He barely registered the voices, the uniforms, the firm pressure guiding him away. **Police.** Someone said something about breaking and entering. Something about consequences.

None of it mattered.

His gaze stayed locked on the still shape under the sheet, on the hands of the doctors who had waited too long.

**He had been right.** And it had changed nothing.

Outside, the wind screamed between the buildings. Inside, the weight of the system crushed the last breath from his lungs.

## Réjean’s Retreat into Pi {#réjean’s-retreat-into-pi}

The prison cell was silent, save for the soft scratch of graphite against paper. The orange notebook—tattered, creased, a lifeline—rested against Réjean’s knee. He flipped through its pages, past the notes on **Konnaxion**, past the sketches of interwoven ideas, until he reached the untouched margins where the real thoughts lived.

Konnaxion. A system of knowledge, collaboration, and creative intelligence. A framework designed to **bridge minds, sharpen ideas, and cut through the inefficiencies of human hesitation**. It was no longer about institutions or hierarchies—it was a direct **convergence of intellect, of effort**. Where bureaucracy failed, Konnaxion **linked people**. **Willed them into synchronicity.**

But it was **only part of the equation**.

On separate, carefully folded pages, Réjean let his mind drift elsewhere—to something more **fundamental**, something **older than any system, any civilization, any mind that had ever questioned the nature of reality**.

**Pi.**

It had started with idle curiosity, tracing circles on the cement wall of his cell. A perfect form. A symbol of balance, harmony, infinity. But all it took was a single cut—a single line dividing it—to create something endless.

**π \= circumference / diameter.**

The ratio was simple. The result? **A number that never ends, never repeats, never settles.**

He stared at the equation, breath slowing. **Perfection, divided, produces infinity.**

A realization took root, something that had been lingering at the edge of his thoughts for years.

**What if Pi wasn’t just a number?**

**What if it was the first calculation? The first division? The first fracture of the universe itself?**

He picked up his pencil again, scrawling the thought before it could dissolve:

*"A circle: timeless, unbroken. But the moment you divide it—carve a single line through its perfection—you introduce the infinite. Perhaps this is more than geometry. Perhaps this is the blueprint of existence itself."*

He hesitated, tapping the pencil against the paper, eyes unfocused.

*"What if 13.7 billion years later, the universe is still calculating Pi—still resolving that first division? What if every atom, every force, every moment, is part of that infinite sequence, spiraling toward something it will never reach?"*

It felt **undeniable**.

He wrote:

*"Pi is older than time. It predates space. It is not contained within the universe; rather, the universe is contained within it."*

His fingers tightened around the pencil.

**Pi is the wound that never closes.** The equation that keeps unfolding. The irreconcilable proof that **perfection, once broken, can never return to itself.**

His breath came slower now, his pulse steady.

He thought of his son.

The equation extended itself beyond numbers, beyond abstraction. It reached into his **own life, his own loss, his own failure to protect what mattered most**. What if everything—even tragedy, even grief—was part of the same **unresolved calculation**?

What if Pi was not just **the infinite**—but the path toward **something whole**?

*"Understanding Pi’s digits might reveal something deeper. If it encodes the structure of reality, perhaps it is the first true language—the silent law beneath all things."*

Yet as he reread his notes, Réjean felt the old frustration creeping in. The weight of knowing something profound but lacking the words to make others see. **He understood. But could he make anyone else understand?**

Tesla had seen the future and died alone. Réjean saw the equation, and here he was—speaking only to paper.

He exhaled, closing the notebook, sliding it beneath the mattress. The questions would still be there tomorrow.

Softly, he whispered to himself:

*"Pi is infinite. Everything is Pi. The circle itself represents perfection before division—timeless, whole, absolute. Pi is the unraveling, and its digits describe reality before the Big Bang, the moment time, space, and existence were born."*

He leaned back, eyes tracing the faint, endless curves he had drawn on the cement.

There was no conclusion. Only the calculation.

## Fractures Beneath the Surface {#fractures-beneath-the-surface}

Outside **Réjean’s confinement**, Québec City carried on. **Cars slid through salted streets, boots crunched over frozen sidewalks, and conversations hummed behind café windows.** To most, nothing had changed. But **beneath the surface, something cracked.** Fractures ran through the foundations of daily life—small, quiet signs of a deeper sickness.

At **École Secondaire Saint-Roch**, the third-period math class dragged on, suffocating in stale repetition. Students slouched over their desks, eyes glazed, notebooks filled with meaningless calculations.

Elias **saw it. He felt it.** He stood at the front of the class, chalk between his fingers, explaining equations no one would remember in a week. **Not because they were incapable—because they weren’t given a reason to care.**

He had once believed in teaching—**real** teaching. But this? This was compliance training. **Memorize, obey, forget.** He would go home tonight, grade papers that measured nothing, then watch another batch of students file in tomorrow, just as unengaged, just as defeated.

He imagined something different. A system that **responded** to students, **molded itself** to their curiosity, their strengths, their struggles. A place where knowledge wasn’t force-fed but uncovered. **But imagining changed nothing.** No such system existed.

---

At a **neat, minimalist office inside Desjardins**, **Thomas Kinsey** stood in front of a projector screen, addressing a group of young entrepreneurs.

They listened carefully, some taking notes, others nodding along, but Thomas **could see past their polite expressions.** He had seen it too many times before—the slow realization creeping in as the numbers settled.

They had ideas. Plans. Some had even **built something already**, clawing their way through the first hurdles. But now, here, they were coming to terms with a truth they hadn’t wanted to admit.

**The system wasn’t built for them.**

Loans came with strings. Investors demanded control. Bureaucracy crushed momentum before ideas even had a chance to breathe. Every dream had a price, and for most, that price was ownership. **A choice: surrender control, or never get off the ground at all.**

Thomas wanted to tell them it would change. That if they worked harder, networked smarter, sacrificed more, they’d get through.

But he knew better.

So he just **kept speaking**, explaining how to play the game **that wasn’t designed for them to win.**

---

At a **small apartment across town**, **Jérémie** sat slouched on his couch, the blue glow of the television casting sharp shadows on the wall. Onscreen, a panel of politicians debated **healthcare reform** at the **Assemblée Nationale**.

Jérémie clenched his jaw, fingers digging into the armrest.

They were guessing. Stumbling through statistics they barely understood, drowning in jargon, **too proud—or too afraid—to admit they had no idea what they were talking about.**

This wasn’t corruption. **This was ignorance.**

They weren’t evil. They were **unprepared.** And yet, here they were—**shaping policies, making decisions that would affect millions.**

Jérémie exhaled sharply.

He imagined something different. **A government that could access real expertise instantly, pulling from those who actually understood the complexities they debated.** A system where **knowledge moved faster than ego, where truth wasn’t twisted into soundbites and empty rhetoric.**

But that world didn’t exist.

So the broadcast continued. And the system **staggered forward, blind, pretending to see.**

---

At **Québec’s courthouse**, **Aïda Eloïse** sat on a stiff bench, her hands resting on a stack of legal documents that had only grown heavier over the past two years.

She checked her watch. Again.

Justice, she thought bitterly, **was a privilege, not a right.**

She had once believed in the system. But each delay, each loophole, each procedural dead-end had eroded that faith.

Here, **justice belonged to those with the money to outlast the process.**

She closed her eyes, took a slow breath.

She imagined something different. A system where **truth wasn’t buried under paperwork**. Where fairness wasn’t reserved for those who could afford it. Where justice **moved**—at the pace of real lives, **not at the pace of bureaucracy.**

But for now, she was still waiting.

---

Everywhere—in **classrooms, banks, courtrooms, and living rooms**—people whispered about the same things.

A world that **no longer worked**.

Education that dulled minds instead of sharpening them. Governments that stumbled through policy-making **like men lost in a storm, too proud to ask for direction.** Entrepreneurs crushed before they could build. Justice that moved **only for those who could afford the wait.**

Society wasn’t collapsing. **It was disconnecting.**

These weren’t revolutions. **They were frustrations. Small, personal, quiet.** The teacher longing for a system that recognized intelligence instead of obedience. The entrepreneur dreaming of financial freedom instead of financial servitude. The citizen waiting for justice that never came. The young idealist watching the government fail in real time.

None of them realized the truth:

**Their struggles weren’t separate.**

They were fractures in the same foundation, **hairline cracks in the same failing system.**

Threaded together by the same absence. The same need. The same quiet, desperate hope that **something—someone—would piece the fragments back together.**

Had Réjean’s ideas existed now—had they spread beyond the walls of his prison—**perhaps those fractures would have found their bridge.**

For now, they remained **invisible fault lines**, growing wider each day.

Waiting.

## **Réjean’s Philosophical Revelation** {#réjean’s-philosophical-revelation}

In the muted solitude of his cell—or perhaps it was now only a small, cramped room; Réjean himself had stopped distinguishing clearly—he sat hunched over his orange notebook. Pages lay spread around him, scribbled wildly with spiraling sequences of Pi, frantic sketches of circles, scattered insights. The outside world had faded, replaced entirely by the compelling, infinite digits unfolding beneath his fingertips.

Réjean's whisper trembled, soft yet charged:

“Circle—perfection, infinite balance. And yet perfection divided…produces Pi. Infinite digits. Eternally unresolved, infinitely irrational.”

He smiled slightly, his eyes widening slowly with wonder and unsettling clarity.

Pi was infinite. Thus, nothing—no galaxy, no universe, no human life—could ever surpass it. Everything had to exist within Pi’s infinite stream. Everything was a digit, a fragment, part of Pi’s eternal calculation. He felt certain that the universe itself, beginning with the first perfect division, circumference by diameter, was still calculating Pi, continuously unfolding itself, digit by digit, across 13.7 billion years.

Réjean whispered softly, gently rocking back and forth, eyes distant, voice barely audible:

"Everything... Everything we experience is Pi still resolving itself. My son’s death…my grief…all inevitable fragments of the universe’s infinite calculation."

He paused, breath quickening slightly, sensing he stood at the threshold of madness or profound genius—though which, he couldn't say. His rational mind strained against the sheer magnitude of the idea, yet he could not deny its elegant simplicity.

"Chaos is essential. Without it, existence stagnates. We fear chaos because we mistake it for disorder—but chaos is simply reality revealing itself, digit by digit, seeking balance through imperfection."

Réjean leaned back slowly, eyes unfocused, whispering quietly into silence:

"The universe began as perfection—a single, infinite circle divided into infinite chaos. And we—humanity, reality itself—are its eternal calculation."

## Subtle Encounter {#subtle-encounter}

The city lay quiet under the weight of winter. Snow muffled the streets, turning every footstep into an echo of something distant, something not quite real.

Réjean walked without direction, without purpose—his thoughts too loud, his body restless. The air tasted sharp, metallic, as if the night itself had edges. The streets felt both familiar and alien, like a place he had known in another life. **Had he been here before? Was he here now?**

Then, beneath a solitary streetlamp, he saw **him**.

A hooded figure sat hunched on a bench, posture loose, gaze alert. **Not begging. Not waiting. Just watching.** The amber light from the lamp barely touched him, as though the shadows bent around his form.

Réjean slowed, uncertain.

The stranger looked up. Eyes calm. Piercing. **Too knowing.**

A silence stretched between them—thick, weighted. Then, softly, the man spoke.

**“What time is it?”**

The question felt absurd, yet Réjean glanced at his watch by instinct. The hands ticked **impossibly**, marking multiple hours at once.

**1, 3, 9, and 11\.**

He blinked. The numbers didn’t shift. They **coexisted**, defying sequence, logic, meaning.

His pulse quickened. “What—?”

The stranger tilted his head, studying him. Then, with a faint smile, he murmured:

**“Sometimes, only chaos opens eyes long shut by comfort. It’s Greek logic.”**

Réjean inhaled sharply. The words landed deep, striking something raw, something he hadn’t yet put into words.

“How do you…?”

The stranger simply stood, **moving without haste, without sound.** Before Réjean could step forward, question further, the figure faded into the darkness beyond the streetlamp—gone as if he had never been.

Réjean remained frozen.

His mind raced. Had the encounter even happened? Had the riddle been spoken aloud, or had it surfaced from within him?

The streetlamp flickered. The night remained unchanged.

And yet, the world did not feel the same.

## **Réjean’s Philosophical Revelation** {#réjean’s-philosophical-revelation-1}

Réjean sat motionless in the soft glow of the solitary lamp, surrounded by pages covered in scrawled numbers, letters, and hastily sketched circles. The small, dim space that confined him—whether still a prison cell or somewhere else entirely, no longer mattered—blurred into insignificance. His thin fingers traced invisible spirals in the air above the paper, eyes wide yet vacant, shimmering between astonishment and confusion.

His notebook lay open before him, overflowing with endless strings of Pi’s digits—numbers flowing into each other endlessly, irrational, infinite. Réjean’s gaze flickered back and forth, fixated and haunted, as though each digit whispered a secret he alone could hear.

With a trembling hand, he scribbled letters beneath numbers, following ancient traditions of Gematria—assigning numeric values to letters, hoping to find meaning within Pi’s endless chaos. Letters formed words, words connected into fragments. First appeared a simple, profound emergence: **"M"**, symbolizing love, unity, or perhaps some primordial force. Then **"Bien"** appeared, meaning goodness, as though emerging naturally from love itself.

Réjean stared in disbelief, heart hammering.

His mind spun wildly between clarity and confusion. He saw clearly the profundity of his discoveries, yet the weight of such revelation crushed him beneath its immensity. How could something so infinite, so significant, reveal itself only to him—a man of painfully modest confidence, always haunted by self-doubt?

He whispered, voice cracking slightly:

"Why me...? Why am I the one to uncover this?"

A sudden wave of dizziness overwhelmed him as the edges of his self—his fragile sense of identity—began softly eroding. His self, never strong or assertive, quietly dissolved, fracturing gently into the infinite depths of Pi’s digits. And yet, simultaneously, Réjean felt a terrifying certainty—that perhaps he was indeed the greatest mind ever to glimpse such profound truth, destined to change human understanding forever.

His hand trembled harder, eyes glazing slightly as letters emerged clearly beneath the numbers:

**M. Bien. Gihecef. Jnon. B0UM.**

Words concealed inside Pi. Simple words, yet brimming with cosmic significance, resonating across language and culture. They hinted at love, goodness, chaos, renewal, creation—forces fundamental to existence itself. Réjean’s breath caught in his throat.

“Why me?” he repeated, more desperately this time. “Why am I the one who sees?”

His breathing quickened, overwhelmed by awe and despair. His sense of self—always fragile—began fragmenting further beneath the unbearable weight of discovery. His identity dissolved into something vast, infinite, unknowable—he lost track of his own thoughts, sinking deeper into a profound abyss of insight.

Slowly, silently, Réjean’s presence faded. The man once known as Réjean McCormick quietly vanished behind a curtain of numbers and letters, quietly swallowed by the infinite spiral of Pi.

From that moment onward, Réjean became unreachable—mysterious, distant, hidden completely behind the veil of his revelations. The man was gone; what remained was only his shadow, silently guiding the world from quiet isolation, becoming little more than an enigmatic presence, impossible to truly know.

## **Scene 1: Thomas’s Frustration with Desjardins** {#scene-1:-thomas’s-frustration-with-desjardins}

Thomas Kinsey sat quietly at the end of the polished table in the luxurious meeting room atop the Desjardins headquarters in Québec City. The room’s panoramic windows overlooked streets dusted with snow, a picturesque contrast to the tense atmosphere inside. Thomas observed silently, frustration simmering beneath his composed expression.

Around him, executives discussed quarterly profits, lavish bonuses, and abstract growth targets—all comfortably detached from the cooperative principles established long ago by Alphonse Desjardins. Thomas glanced briefly at the old founder’s portrait on the wall, feeling a pang of shame at the profound disconnect between ideal and reality.

Finally, unable to hold back any longer, Thomas cleared his throat, speaking politely yet firmly:

“Are we certain these substantial executive bonuses align with our cooperative values? Many of our members are struggling. Shouldn’t our resources support them first?”

Silence settled awkwardly. Heads turned toward him slowly, expressions politely condescending.

The Chairman responded dismissively, voice carefully controlled:

“Thomas, the board has approved these measures. We’re aware of your concerns, but these decisions are necessary to attract executive talent. This is simply the reality of the modern financial environment.”

Thomas’s jaw tightened subtly, eyes reflecting quiet frustration. Privately, he resolved in that moment to quietly challenge the system from within—returning Desjardins to its cooperative roots, even if it meant silent, strategic confrontation.

## **Scene 2: Simon’s Vision of Transparency** {#scene-2:-simon’s-vision-of-transparency}

Late at night, in a cluttered tech startup office illuminated by dozens of glowing screens, Simon Sotaniram stood before a small group of tired but enthusiastic developers. Diagrams and software sketches plastered the walls, code and notes sprawling chaotically around them.

Simon—intense, driven, haunted by memories he rarely spoke of—was demonstrating an early prototype of his transparency and workflow system. The tool was self-contained, efficient, intuitive—built explicitly to dismantle opaque bureaucratic structures.

“This isn't just software,” Simon spoke passionately, his eyes blazing with conviction. “It's freedom from institutional corruption. Imagine governments, schools, or businesses operating without delays or excuses—where everything reaches exactly the right person, instantly, transparently. Imagine a society free from lies and inefficiency.”

He paused briefly, a shadow crossing his face, hinting at old wounds that drove his determination.

“We've all seen corruption hide behind opacity. This ends now. We must build this. We must make it perfect. People need this—urgently.”

The small team watched in silence, deeply moved by Simon’s sincerity, quietly inspired by his fervent belief that their work could change society fundamentally.

## **Scene 3: Jérémie’s Artistic Provocation** {#scene-3:-jérémie’s-artistic-provocation}

Inside a brightly lit dance studio in Québec City, Jérémie Lemoine spun across the floor, sweat glistening on his forehead, movements fierce, provocative, charged with raw emotion. Jérémie’s choreography explicitly confronted social injustice and systemic corruption—boldly daring audiences to face uncomfortable truths they preferred to ignore.

“Jérémie,” the theater director interrupted sharply, frowning, “this is too political—too risky. Art should inspire, not accuse.”

Jérémie paused, breathing heavily, his eyes fiercely defiant.

“Art that doesn’t confront injustice isn’t art—it’s decoration,” he replied softly, voice vibrating with passionate certainty. Privately, Jérémie already knew he would stage the piece exactly as intended, regardless of institutional pressure.

## **Scene 4: Aïda’s Quiet Ethical Leadership** {#scene-4:-aïda’s-quiet-ethical-leadership}

In a small community center across town, a circle of quiet, thoughtful individuals sat listening closely as Aïda Éloïse spoke. A soft-spoken yet profoundly charismatic woman, she discussed ethics, responsibility, collective governance, and the necessity of ecological stewardship.

“We must build communities rooted in deeper values than profit and power,” she explained calmly. “We need mutual care, transparency, and ethical leadership. True change arises quietly—within ourselves first, then outward.”

Listeners nodded silently, deeply moved by her words. Among them, Jérémie Lemoine quietly absorbed her message, recognizing philosophical truths he could incorporate into his provocative performances. Aïda, sensing their quiet agreement, nodded gently, knowing seeds of change had begun taking root.

## Scene 5: Elias’s Equilibrium {#scene-5:-elias’s-equilibrium}

The fire crackled softly, its glow casting long shadows along the wooden walls of the small cabin. The air smelled of pine and slow-burning embers. Outside, the trees stood silent beneath the weight of winter, their branches bending slightly, but never breaking.

Elias sat alone, hands wrapped around a warm mug, eyes tracing the flickering movement of the flames. **Here, in solitude, the noise of the day unraveled.**

The weight of his students’ quiet struggles still lingered. The blank stares, the restless hands tapping against desks, the ones who wanted to care but couldn’t—**or wouldn’t**. ## The Last Laugh {#the-last-laugh}

The auditorium erupted in applause as Vladimir Poutine stepped aside, extending a respectful hand toward the man emerging from the wings. Réjean McCormick walked forward quietly, his steps steady but humble, his expression serene beneath the enigmatic mask of King Klown.

As he reached the podium, he paused, his presence commanding in the heavy silence. Then, with a slow, deliberate motion, he raised his hands—and clapped.

The stage lights dimmed.

A ripple of intrigue passed through the audience. Murmurs stirred among the gathered world leaders, journalists, and dignitaries. What was he planning?

Then, with effortless ease, Réjean reached behind his ear and pulled out a **cannabis joint**.

The audience’s reaction was immediate—some gasped, some chuckled, and others leaned forward, intrigued. Without hesitation, he flicked open a lighter, the flame casting a warm glow across his face. He touched it to the end of the joint and inhaled slowly, the ember glowing red in the dimmed light.

A visible shift spread across the auditorium.

Some members of the audience **burst into laughter and applause**, others exchanged bewildered glances, while a few sat stiff, their expressions frozen in a mix of shock and disbelief. The subtle, unmistakable scent of cannabis began weaving through the air.

Vladimir Poutine, sitting at the front, stood abruptly.

The room **hushed**.

His gaze was stern, unreadable. He reached for a glass of water from the table beside him, took a measured step toward the podium… and with no warning—

**Threw the entire glass of water directly into Réjean’s face.**

A collective gasp filled the room.

Réjean **froze**, blinking as water dripped down his face, soaking into his shirt. The room tensed—was this about to turn into a diplomatic scandal? Would this moment define the absurd end to what had been a profound journey?

A heartbeat passed.

Then another.

And then—**Réjean started laughing**.

Deep, **uproarious** laughter.

Vladimir’s expression remained stony for a fraction of a second longer—before his face cracked into a wide grin. Suddenly, **he too burst into laughter**.

The tension **shattered**.

The room, stunned just moments before, erupted into relieved, **raucous laughter and applause**.

With an easy camaraderie, Vladimir stepped up beside Réjean and threw an arm around his **still-drenched** shoulders. Réjean, still grinning, slung his own arm over Vladimir’s in return. The two men **exited the stage together**, playfully nudging one another, their laughter still echoing as they disappeared into the wings.

The final image the world would remember?

Two supposed adversaries, **united not by politics, not by power—but by humor**.

And in that moment, the world understood:

**Peace wasn’t just about treaties. It was about connection.**

# **Final Flashback: The Infinite Fight** {#final-flashback:-the-infinite-fight}

The **church loomed behind them**, a dark silhouette against the deep violet of the night sky. Its stained-glass windows reflected the distant glow of the city, fractured light casting strange shapes onto the wet pavement. The cemetery stretched into the darkness beside it, rows of nameless stones swallowed by the cold mist.

Simon’s **breath was heavy**, each inhale sharp, controlled—barely. His fists **ached**, clenched so tight his nails pressed into his palms, but the pain barely registered.

The rage was louder.

It wasn’t just about Orgo. Not just about the media storm that had **shredded his name to pieces**.

It was about **Réjean**.

Réjean, who had built the idea. Who had convinced him to be **the face of it**. Who had handed him the **front row** of a vision so grand—only to **step back** the moment it started to collapse.

And now, **he stood there**, rolling his shoulders like this was just another night.

*"You set me up,"* Simon muttered, voice raw.

Réjean **watched him**, unreadable. *"I put you where you belonged."*

Simon let out a short, sharp laugh, but there was no humor in it.

*"The front?"* His voice edged toward a growl. *"You mean the line of fire."*

Réjean shrugged. *"You believed in Orgo. You spoke for it better than I ever could."*

*"Yeah? And where were you when it all came crashing down?"* Simon’s voice rose. *"When the whole world was calling me a fraud, a monster—where were you?"*

Réjean’s expression didn’t shift. *"You’re still standing."*

Something **snapped**.

Simon **moved first**, his fist flying forward—**Réjean dodged just before impact**, barely shifting, letting the punch slice past him.

He **smirked.**

*"Come on,"* he muttered, cracking his knuckles. *"You want to break something? Break me."*

Simon’s **jaw tightened**, his chest **heaving**.

*"You think this is a joke?"*

*"No joke, mon frère."* Réjean’s voice was **calm**, too calm. *"You’re already fighting in your head. Might as well do it for real."*

Simon **charged**.

And the fight **began.**

Simon didn’t hesitate. He surged forward, swinging hard, aiming for Réjean’s ribs. But Réjean wasn’t just dodging tonight. He stepped in, catching Simon’s forearm, twisting his body as he slammed an elbow into Simon’s chest.

Simon staggered back, breath knocked from his lungs, but he recovered fast. He threw a sharp left hook—Réjean barely ducked in time. The impact grazed his temple, sending a shock through his skull. But instead of retreating, Réjean drove a knee into Simon’s side, forcing a grunt from his throat.

Simon grinned through clenched teeth. “So you’re finally fighting back.”

Réjean wiped blood from his lip. “Wouldn’t be much of a lesson otherwise.”

They crashed into each other again, bodies colliding like a storm. Simon’s fists found flesh, Réjean’s strikes found bone. Every hit sent a ripple of pain through their bodies, but neither stopped. They fought like it meant something—because it did.

A brutal right from Simon cracked against Réjean’s jaw, sending him stumbling against the iron fence of the cemetery. The clang of metal echoed in the night. Réjean shook his head, blinking away stars, then pushed off the fence and slammed his forehead into Simon’s.

The impact sent them both reeling.

Simon wiped at his forehead, his fingers coming away bloody. He coughed out a laugh. “That all you got?”

Réjean rolled his shoulders, pain radiating from his bruised ribs. “Not even close.”

He lunged. His fist met Simon’s stomach, forcing the air from his lungs. Simon doubled over, and Réjean followed with an uppercut that snapped his head back. Simon stumbled, but instead of retreating, he gritted his teeth and came back swinging.

They hit the ground, rolling through the dirt and fallen leaves, fists colliding, breath ragged. They weren’t just trying to hurt each other—they were breaking something deeper. The weight of the world, the frustration, the betrayal, the unanswered questions.

The fight slowed. Their bodies burned, their lungs ached. Blood dripped from their brows, from split lips and torn knuckles.

Simon sat up first, resting his forearms on his knees, chest heaving. Réjean stayed on his back, staring up at the sky, his face painted with sweat and blood.

The cold night wrapped around them. The church bells rang in the distance.

Simon exhaled. “Feel better?”

Réjean chuckled, wincing as he sat up. “Not really. But you needed that.”

Simon scoffed. “I think I broke a rib.”

Réjean grinned, spitting blood onto the ground. “Then we’re even.”

The night stretched between them, silent except for their breathing. Then, finally, Réjean looked over, his eyes catching something distant, something beyond the pain.

Simon wiped his mouth, tasting blood. “You always did have a way of pissing me off.”

Réjean smirked. “That’s the point. You needed to get it out.” He stretched, wincing slightly. “But let’s be real. If you *really* wanted to hurt me, you wouldn’t have pulled your last punch.”

Simon scoffed, shaking his head. The rage had burned through him, leaving behind something raw but cleaner. “So what now? You tell me I have to keep fighting? That I have to ‘find my own truth’ or some other philosophical bullshit?”

Réjean chuckled, rubbing his bruised ribs. “Not this time.” He moved toward a wooden table at the edge of the cemetery, pulling out it’s orange notebook. “You ever wonder why we’re still here? You and me?”

Simon narrowed his eyes. “You’re about to go off again, aren’t you?”

Réjean shrugged. “Always.” He picked up a piece of paper, covered in numbers and annotations. “I’ve been thinking a lot about Pi. About why it’s infinite, why it never repeats.”

Simon exhaled sharply, running a hand through his sweat-drenched hair. “Christ, Réjean. You and your numbers.”

Réjean ignored the jab. “You see, Pi isn’t just some endless stream of digits. It’s the mark of something deeper. A fundamental *code* that shapes reality itself.” He held up the paper, the numbers scrawled across it like an ancient script. “And I think I found something in it.”

Simon frowned but didn’t walk away.

Réjean continued, his voice quieter now, like he was revealing a secret. “People think Pi is random, that its digits are just noise. But what if they’re not? What if they encode something?” He tapped the page. “What if they tell a story? Not just a mathematical curiosity, but a *narrative*—a blueprint of the universe itself?”

Simon crossed his arms, skeptical but listening.

“I ran the numbers through a different lens—numerology, linguistic mapping. And I started seeing words. Not gibberish. *Concepts.* Love. Goodness. Chaos. Creation.”

Simon exhaled slowly. “You’re telling me the universe is spelled out in Pi?”

Réjean’s eyes glowed with intensity. “I’m telling you that Pi might not just describe the universe—it might *be* the universe. A cosmic equation embedded in a single number. A cycle of division and return.” He tossed the paper onto the table. “Think about it. A circle, the most perfect shape, divided gives us Pi. And yet, that division doesn’t resolve. It goes on forever, searching for completion it will never reach.”

Simon stared at the numbers, the ink smudged where Réjean’s fingers had pressed too hard.

Réjean tapped the ground beside him, his fingers tracing invisible numbers in the dirt. His breath was uneven, but his voice was steady.

“It starts with **M**—thirteen in the sequence. The letter for **Love**. The first principle of the universe isn’t force, isn’t destruction—it’s the pull toward unity. The need to reconnect what was divided.”

His finger moved across the numbers. “Then comes **Bien—Good**. Not just an idea, but a direction. A purpose. Love seeks Good. The universe isn’t random—it has intent, an unfolding order hidden in what looks like chaos.”

He tapped the next section. “Then **Gihecef**—the split. Duality. The moment the first unity fractures into opposing forces. Masculine and feminine. Light and dark. Creation and destruction. Without opposition, there’s no motion, no progress.”

His breath misted in the cold air. “And then, **Jnon**—Renewal. The cycle beginning again. The universe doesn’t just expand—it breathes. It pauses before it leaps forward, like a pendulum reaching its peak before swinging back.”

Finally, he pressed his hand firmly against the last word. “**B0UM.** The Big Bang. The shattering, the ignition. But it wasn’t just an explosion—it was part of the pattern. A consequence. A return. The first breath of the universe wasn’t chaos—it was memory.”

He exhaled, staring at the numbers. “Pi isn’t just a number. It’s the equation of existence. The fracture and the pull. The infinite attempt to close the circle.”

Simon frowned, his fingers tightening around the bloodstained page. "And you think this means something?"

Réjean’s smirk returned, but this time, there was no arrogance—just certainty. "I think we’ve been asking the wrong questions. We keep looking for meaning in words, in gods, in history. But the universe isn’t speaking in scripture, Simon. It’s speaking in **numbers**."

Simon let out a slow breath, his body aching from the fight, his mind heavier still.

Réjean continued, his voice lower now, almost reverent. "We treat Pi like a tool, a mathematical curiosity, but what if it’s not? What if it’s a memory? A scar left behind by something greater? A blueprint, not just of physics, but of intention?" He tapped the paper again. "This isn’t randomness. It’s a cycle. A logic so vast, we mistake it for chaos."

Simon ran a hand down his face, shaking his head. "So what the hell does that mean for me?"

Réjean exhaled, staring at the sky above the graveyard. "It means everything repeats. Every fracture leads back to something whole. Every loss is part of the equation. And you?" He looked at Simon. "You don’t stop swinging. You don’t stop searching. You don’t stop fighting."

Simon let out a tired, bitter laugh. "You’re waiting for them to carve out a **new** Nobel Prize category for you, huh? Something between physics and philosophy? ‘Most Unhinged Metaphysical Revelation of the Year’?"

Réjean grinned. "It’s overdue."

Simon chuckled, wincing at the pain in his ribs. "Christ, Réjean. You think you’re rewriting the universe like some second coming?"

Réjean stretched, blood still drying on his knuckles. "Nah," he said, leaning back against the cold iron fence. "Just reading what was always there."

Simon scoffed, shaking his head. "You really are insane."

Réjean smirked, closing his eyes. "I know."

The night stretched around them, the wind whistling between the gravestones. Above, the stars held their silence, indifferent and infinite, while two battered men laughed at the weight of it all.
