# Canonical State — Version 2

**Status:** Highest-authority document in the book-definition pack.

This file prevents an AI author from confusing:
- a long historical note with a current decision;
- an experimental idea with a canonical principle;
- an intended implementation with a verified implementation;
- a narrative device with evidence;
- a local formula with a book-level relation.

When lower-level documents conflict with this file, **this file wins** until the project is explicitly versioned again.

## 1. Book identity

**Title:** *Civilizational Coherence*  
**Subtitle:** *From Distributed Intelligence to Cumulative Collective Agency*

The book is a serious nonfiction research/design argument. It is not a manifesto, kOA manual, fiction book, general AI book, general history of civilization, or theory of everything.

## 2. Canonical thesis

A civilization can possess enormous distributed intelligence, competence, judgment, institutions, resources and initiative while losing a consequential fraction of that potential in the transitions required for collective action.

\[
DistributedPotential
\rightarrow
Coherence
\rightarrow
CollectiveAgency
\rightarrow
Learning
\rightarrow
FutureCapacity
\]

The intellectual center of the book is in the **arrows**.

## 3. Distributed intelligence vs distributed potential

\[
DistributedIntelligence \subset DistributedPotential
\]

**Distributed intelligence** refers primarily to distributed knowledge, perception, expertise and judgment.

**Distributed potential** is wider and also includes practical skill, experience, material resources, institutional capability, initiative and operational capacity.

The title uses *Distributed Intelligence* for clarity. The theory often uses *Distributed Potential* because the causal model is broader. Do not treat them as exact synonyms.

## 4. Canonical definition of civilizational coherence

Civilizational coherence is the capacity of a large, heterogeneous social system to make its distributed knowledge, competence, judgment, resources and initiative mutually discoverable and functionally composable, so that they can be converted into coordinated action and, through feedback and memory, into greater future collective capacity.

\[
Coherence \neq Consensus
\]

\[
Coherence \neq Decentralization
\]

\[
FunctionalCentrality \neq Domination
\]

\[
CentralHub \neq CentralizedSovereignty
\]

\[
Coherence \neq MaximumConnectivity
\]

\[
Coherence \neq Homogeneity
\]

\[
Coherence \neq Goodness
\]

Coherence is dynamic, contextual, multidimensional, compatible with pluralism and local autonomy, and potentially dangerous if coordination improves faster than correction.

## 5. Four project layers

\[
Theory \neq Architecture \neq Narrative \neq Practice
\]

### Theory
Explains the general problem, mechanisms, hypotheses and design requirements.

### Architecture
Attempts to instantiate those requirements. kOA is the recurring architectural laboratory.

### Narrative
Makes complex structure perceptible, memorable, criticizable and culturally transmissible. The Surreal / King Klown material is one implementation of this function.

### Practice / Mastery
The human and institutional capacity to operate, maintain, teach, repair, govern and evolve what has been built.

Possible project loop:

\[
Theory
\rightarrow
Architecture
\rightarrow
NarrativeLegibility
\rightarrow
Practice/Mastery
\]

with feedback:

\[
Practice
\rightarrow
Failure
\rightarrow
Memory/Narrative
\rightarrow
TheoryRevision
\rightarrow
ArchitectureRevision
\]

These are conceptual maps, not empirical laws.

## 5A. Centrality and articulation stance

Civilizational coherence does **not** require the absence of a center. The current theory explicitly allows — and the kOA laboratory intentionally seeks — a strong **articulation hub** that makes distributed capacities easier to discover, interpret, combine and route.

Separate:

- functional centrality;
- centralization of control;
- centralization of sovereignty;
- terminal dependency.

\[
MaximumUsefulCentrality + MinimumTerminalDependency
\]

**Status:** design heuristic, not a calibrated objective function.

A strong hub may centralize access, navigation, contextual surfacing, composition and legibility while expertise, execution, ownership and legitimate authority remain distributed or scale-specific.

\[
StrongHub + DistributedCapability + LocalExecution + ContestableAuthority + ReplicableWhole
\]

Replication means a complete coherent hub can be reproduced and become another center. Federation may then connect strong centers to strong centers. Polycentricity therefore does not imply weak or centerless institutions.

## 6. kOA status

**Status:** concrete architectural laboratory / implementation family.

kOA is not:
- the premise of the theory;
- proof of the theory;
- the only valid implementation;
- a sovereign solution for civilization.

Removal test:

> If every reference to kOA were removed from a chapter, the chapter's main theoretical argument should remain substantially valid.

If another architecture satisfies the derived requirements better, the intellectual program should prefer the better architecture.

## 7. Current intended architectural stance

Unless current implementation evidence establishes otherwise, describe these as **CURRENT INTENDED ARCHITECTURE / DESIGN TARGET**, not verified fact:

- a strong coherent hub / coordination spine as the normal user-facing articulation point;
- Kintsugi-style integration of selected capabilities under shared contracts rather than silo duplication;
- local core operation;
- optional federation;
- portable/forkable knowledge artifacts;
- contextual competence rather than universal human rank;
- deterministic canonical semantic/language realization where specified;
- AI-optional canonical runtime;
- offline capability for functions that do not intrinsically require network access;
- separable knowledge artifacts and coordination software;
- independent instances sharing initial compatibility by origin rather than runtime central dependency.

\[
BuiltWithAI \neq PoweredByAI
\]

Current intended direction:

\[
AI\text{-}assisted\ construction
\rightarrow
ValidatedDeterministicArtifact
\rightarrow
AI\text{-}independent\ canonical\ operation
\]

## 8. Capture stance

Never claim capture is impossible.

Canonical design aim:

> make capture non-terminal, visible, contestable and bypassable.

\[
InstanceCapture \neq SystemCapture
\]

under conditions of genuine portability, independent operation, forkability, substitutability and social legitimacy.

Technical forkability does not imply social forkability.

## 9. Credibility / EkoH stance

**Accepted design principle:** contextual competence and portable evidence.

Avoid a universal immutable score of human worth.

\[
Evidence/Attestations
\rightarrow
ContextualCompetenceReading
\]

with:

\[
StableEvidence + RevisableInterpretation
\]

### Experimental proposal
Expose some low-recognition/general participants to selected questions before established experts, then compare repeated responses with later reference judgments and, where possible, outcomes as one possible signal of latent competence.

**Status:** EXPERIMENTAL PROPOSAL.

Agreement with expert consensus is not truth; without outcome validation it primarily measures alignment with a reference group.

## 10. Smart Vote stance

Canonical architectural principles:
- participation records/facts remain distinct from readings;
- multiple lenses can operate over the same underlying record;
- no single lens is sovereign;
- lens rules should be inspectable, versioned and contestable;
- recommended/default lenses create attention power;
- expertise-weighted readings do not create political sovereignty.

\[
Facts \neq Readings
\]

\[
Expertise \neq Sovereignty
\]

## 11. Integrity / sanctions stance

Sanctions should be tied to demonstrated procedural violations such as fraud, coercion, identity abuse, hidden relevant conflicts or manipulation.

Consequences should be contextual, evidence-based, appealable, versioned and potentially time-bounded where appropriate.

Do not convert general political or moral disagreement into a universal integrity penalty.

## 12. Knowledge / Kristal stance

\[
Kristal \neq Konnaxion
\]

Kristal is the portable/forkable/versioned knowledge-artifact concept. Konnaxion is coordination/governance/intelligence mechanics.

\[
ClaimExists \neq ClaimIsValid \neq InstitutionalEndorsement
\]

A Kristal can represent scientific consensus, minority hypotheses, religion, fiction or fantasy worlds without implying system endorsement.

## 13. Semantic stance

\[
Meaning
\rightarrow
StructuredRepresentation
\rightarrow
LanguageSpecificRealization
\]

Requirements:
- no hidden mandatory natural-language pivot as semantic truth;
- preserve non-equivalence;
- preserve ambiguity where needed;
- permit local ontologies;
- mappings have provenance/status/version;
- deterministic realization does not imply semantic correctness.

## 14. Advice and authority stance

Never collapse:

\[
Evidence \neq Advice \neq Decision \neq Execution
\]

and:

\[
Competence \neq Mandate
\]

The expert channel can be strong, visible and accountable without becoming sovereign.

A legitimate authority may disagree with expert advice; preserve the advice, decision rationale and later outcomes so both expert and political judgment can be evaluated.

## 15. Federation / sovereignty stance

Replication conceptually precedes federation.

\[
Replication
\rightarrow
IndependentOperation
\rightarrow
OptionalFederation
\]

Federation is not evidence of central dependence.

Local functions should survive federation-link loss when they do not intrinsically require federation.

Information, authority and execution can occur at different scales.

## 16. Open-good stance

**General concept:** open good is broader than open source; enough of the functional good should be portable to support independent use where rights allow.

**Project-specific constraint:** the original public-good UCKK/Koali-oriented reference platform is intended **not to be monetized as the public good itself**. It may be duplicated, adapted and instantiated under other contexts. Do not universalize this project constraint into a theorem about all implementations.

## 17. Seeded simulation stance

A platform may bootstrap structured public debate using synthetic simulations derived from public sources.

Required labels:
- verbatim source;
- structured paraphrase;
- synthetic simulation;
- content validated by the real person.

The real person may later authenticate, correct and, where the system permits, take control of the representation.

Later changes of position should be represented as changes, not silent historical rewriting.

Synthetic simulation must never appear as an authentic statement.

## 18. Narrative / Surreal stance

General theory uses:
- **legible coherence**;
- **narrative mediation**;
- **narrative memory**;
- **cultural contestability**.

The Surreal is a project-specific narrative method, not a universal requirement.

Narrative functions currently recognized:
1. manifestation of the whole;
2. orientation;
3. mnemonic encoding;
4. attention/mobilization;
5. adversarial simulation;
6. normative precommitment;
7. founder decentering.

\[
NarrativeCoherence \neq StructuralCoherence
\]

The narrative must be forkable/rethemeable. King Klown is not a mandatory technical or institutional component.

## 19. Founder stance

The founder is not evidence and must not become a permanent dependency.

Independent-reproduction test: can people outside the founding group understand, reproduce, install, operate, maintain, repair, govern, teach, fork, adapt or replace the relevant system/model without unique founder knowledge or continuing founder permission? This does not require a creator-owned channel to become founderless or to survive its creator; it tests whether capability and institutional form can exist independently of exclusive founder dependence.

The narrative should equip future participants to criticize the founder if the founder violates the stated invariants.

## 20. Measurement stance

Do not assume one civilizational coherence score.

Treat coherence as a latent or multidimensional property inferred from multiple indicators.

Primary success chain:

\[
Participation
\rightarrow
KnowledgeQuality
\rightarrow
Reuse
\rightarrow
Decision
\rightarrow
Action
\rightarrow
Outcome
\rightarrow
Learning
\]

Adoption alone is not success.

## 21. Falsifiability stance

The theory must be able to fail.

Possible failure:
- adoption rises but quality/reuse/outcomes do not;
- coordination costs exceed gains;
- functional centrality turns into opaque domination or terminal dependency;
- expert participation does not materialize;
- portability is nominal rather than practical;
- social recovery after capture is too slow;
- compatibility decays too rapidly;
- narrative creates loyalty rather than critique;
- mastery remains founder-dependent.

Do not redefine every negative result as confirmation.
