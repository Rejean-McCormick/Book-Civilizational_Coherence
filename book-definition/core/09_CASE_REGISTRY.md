## Independent reproduction / founder non-dependence

Test whether people outside the founding group can:
- install or reproduce;
- repair;
- update;
- govern their own instance/institution;
- explain;
- teach;
- fork or retheme;
without unique founder knowledge or continuing founder permission.

A creator-owned original channel may continue to depend on the creator by choice; the test concerns whether capability and derivative institutions can exist independently.

Best chapters: 8, 23, 25.

## Git analogy

Useful for versioning, lineage, fork, merge and delta.

Boundary:
Knowledge, institutions and legitimacy are not code. Social forks carry political/cultural costs absent from repository branching.

## Internet protocols / standards analogy

Useful for interoperability, stable contracts and composition without common ownership.

Boundary:
Human institutions carry authority, incentives, coercion and meaning.

## Markets analogy

Useful for distributed coordination and local knowledge.

Boundary:
Not every relevant value is priced; power and externalities matter.

## Scientific institutions analogy

Useful for distributed expertise, peer criticism, consensus, replication and cumulative memory.

Boundary:
Science is not a complete model of democratic authority or execution.

## Rule for all examples

Classify examples as:
- illustration;
- analogy;
- historical case;
- empirical evidence;
- hypothetical stress test.

Never let a hypothetical stress test silently become evidence.
