# Style Guide

## Voice

Serious, analytical, accessible, calm.

The manuscript should feel like an intelligent investigation: confident in distinctions, willing to revise conclusions.

Avoid the tone of:
- manifesto;
- startup pitch;
- prophecy;
- software documentation;
- academic jargon performance.

## Language

Default manuscript language: **French**.

Keep established English chapter titles unless editorial strategy changes.

Use English technical terms where canonical or more precise, but define them at first use.

## Paragraph architecture

Prefer cohesive paragraphs over stacked one-line declarations.

A difficult concept benefits from:
1. claim;
2. explanation;
3. example;
4. limitation.

Lists are useful for taxonomies and tests, not default prose.

## Openings

Begin chapters with a paradox, scene, familiar institutional failure or concrete question.

Do not begin with abstract systems terminology unless already earned.

## Definitions

Motivate the problem before naming the term.

## Formulas

Display only formulas worth remembering.

Every formula needs:
- literal meaning;
- conceptual meaning;
- status.

Never leave notation as decoration.

## Examples

After abstraction, move quickly to a concrete example.

Preferred recurring domains:
- emerging agricultural disease;
- hospital/public-health coordination;
- scientific dispute;
- infrastructure project;
- municipality;
- organizational memory;
- captured public instance.

Examples expose mechanisms; they do not prove universality.

## Metaphors

Useful metaphors:
- bottleneck;
- bridge;
- interface;
- fork;
- memory;
- routing.

State limits when an analogy can mislead.

## Narrative material

King Klown/Surreal should appear as:
- design case;
- cultural interface;
- mnemonic encoding;
- adversarial simulation.

The theory must remain legible without the narrative.

## kOA tone

Use:
- "attempts to";
- "is designed to";
- "provides one architecture for";
- "in the intended architecture";
- "as an implementation hypothesis".

Avoid:
- "solves";
- "guarantees";
- "eliminates capture";
- "revolutionary";
unless independently demonstrated and necessary.

## Citations

Source external factual claims without citation saturation.

Conceptual arguments do not need decorative citations.

## Density

Prefer:
- one central distinction per movement;
- one main formula per chapter, often fewer;
- one full worked example;
- several short counterexamples.

## Repetition

Use intentional recurrence, not duplication.

Example:
> "Chapter 7 separated competence from status. The present problem is what happens when that distinction controls visibility..."

Do not reteach Chapter 7.

## Section headings

Headings should carry argument.

Prefer:
- "The Number That Hides the System"
- "Freedom to Be Wrong"
- "When the Platform Dies"

Avoid generic administrative labels.

## Endings

A chapter should end by making the next chapter necessary:
1. what was established;
2. what remains unresolved;
3. next question.

## Reader relationship

Do not instruct the reader to admire the project.

Invite scrutiny.

A reader may accept the diagnosis, accept some design principles and reject kOA. That is a legitimate outcome.
