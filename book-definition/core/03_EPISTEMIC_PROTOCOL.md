# Epistemic Protocol

The book proposes a research program. Credibility depends on disciplined separation among observations, interpretations, hypotheses, values and implementations.

## Claim classes

### Fact
Sourceable external observation or established result. Facts need sources.

### Interpretation
Proposed explanation or framing of facts. Interpretations need argument and, where relevant, competing readings.

### Hypothesis
Claim that predicts or expects an observable difference. Hypotheses need possible failure conditions.

### Design principle
Property intentionally sought. It needs rationale and tradeoffs, not false empirical certainty.

### Implementation
Concrete mechanism. It can be replaced while the principle survives.

### Normative commitment
Value choice not derivable from efficiency: non-domination, privacy, political equality in relevant contexts, legitimate refusal.

## Claim hierarchy

\[
Definition
\rightarrow
ConceptualRelation
\rightarrow
Hypothesis
\rightarrow
DesignPrinciple
\rightarrow
Implementation
\]

These are different burdens of justification, not a ladder of truth.

## Evidence burden

\[
ClaimStrength\uparrow
\Rightarrow
EvidenceBurden\uparrow
\]

A narrow workflow claim needs narrower evidence than a civilization-wide causal claim.

Prefer:
- "is designed to";
- "should, under these conditions";
- "the hypothesis is";
- "one plausible effect is";

over unsupported certainty.

## No hidden precision

For:

\[
A_t=P_t-L_t
\]

do not imply that potential and losses are already measured in one unit. The formula encodes a causal intuition.

## Evidence and endorsement

\[
Claim \neq Source \neq Validation
\]

\[
InstitutionalValidation \neq Truth
\]

Recognition always has scope and provenance.

## Existence and validity

\[
ClaimExists \neq ClaimIsValid
\]

A plural knowledge system may represent false claims, minority hypotheses, religion, fiction and counterfactuals without implying equal evidence or endorsement.

## Unknown is valid

The system and the book must be able to say:
- unknown;
- unresolved;
- insufficient evidence;
- ambiguous;
- contested.

A framework forced to always output one answer is pressured to manufacture certainty.

## Sources

For manuscript research prefer:
- peer-reviewed research;
- official standards;
- primary institutional documentation;
- credible historical sources;
- high-quality syntheses.

Do not use kOA documents as evidence for broad claims about the world. They may document intended implementation.

## Causal language

Distinguish:
- correlation;
- plausible mechanism;
- supported causal effect;
- conceptual dependency.

A relation like:

\[
Portability\uparrow \rightarrow Reuse\uparrow
\]

is a hypothesis until evidence supports it in context.

## Historical analogies

Avoid morality tales:
- Galileo does not prove minorities are right;
- open source does not prove decentralization always wins;
- blockchain does not automatically solve credibility.

Use history to expose design tensions.

## Implementation state

Use labels when needed:
- implemented and verified;
- implemented but not independently validated;
- intended/final architecture or design target.

If documents conflict, do not silently reconcile.

## Falsifiability

Every major empirical hypothesis should answer:
- what would we expect if true?
- what comparison matters?
- what would count against it?
- what result would narrow scope?
- what result would justify abandonment?

Allow failure to localize:
- mechanism failure;
- implementation failure;
- scale failure;
- governance failure;
- adoption failure;
- theory failure.

## Architecture is not outcome evidence

\[
ArchitectureExists \neq HypothesisValidated
\]

An implementation may demonstrate feasibility without demonstrating superior outcomes.

## Narrative epistemics

\[
Narrative \neq Evidence
\]

Fiction may simulate a failure mode or make an invariant memorable. It does not establish likelihood or effectiveness.

Narrative claims about reality must resolve back into ordinary evidence.

## Final posture

Read the book as:
> a set of distinctions, mechanisms, design hypotheses and concrete architectures whose value depends on what survives comparison, implementation and empirical correction.

## Centrality claims

Treat claims such as "a unified articulation hub can surface more relevant capability than conventional search/social fragmentation" as **empirical hypotheses** until benchmarked.

Treat `MaximumUsefulCentrality + MinimumTerminalDependency` as a **design principle**, not a scientific law.

Treat Konnaxion/Kintsugi descriptions as implementation/documentation claims that still require code/test verification before being written as demonstrated performance.
