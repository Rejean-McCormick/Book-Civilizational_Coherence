# Chapter 15 Brief — Expert Advice and Democratic Authority

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Relevant expert advice is now visible to legitimate decision-makers.

## Proof obligation
Preserve strong expert advice without transferring democratic sovereignty to experts.

## Main mechanism
Evidence supports advice; advice informs but does not command legitimate authority; decisions and reasons are recorded and compared with outcomes.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Evidence ≠ Advice ≠ Decision
- Expertise ≠ Sovereignty
- Political Equality ≠ Epistemic Equivalence
- Legitimacy ≠ Outcome Quality
- Epistemic Confidence ≠ Recommendation Strength

## Best worked example
Experts recommend A while an elected government chooses B for explicit normative/distributional reasons.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
Advisory systems can become technocratic power centers or ceremonial theater. Experts can be wrong; governments can cherry-pick.

## kOA role
Parallel advisory channel using EkoH/Smart Vote/ethiKos; explicit decision boundary; outcome memory.

## Epistemic status
Advice/authority separation is normative-institutional in democratic contexts; accountability effects are hypotheses.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Execution mechanics.

## Exit condition
Even a legitimate, informed decision can remain symbolic. How does it become coordinated action?
