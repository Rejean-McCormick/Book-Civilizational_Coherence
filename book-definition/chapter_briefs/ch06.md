# Chapter 6 Brief — Competition and Collaboration as Infrastructural Regimes

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Systems reproduce behavior through incentives and accumulated structure.

## Proof obligation
Treat competition and collaboration as infrastructural regimes rather than moral opposites.

## Main mechanism
Regimes become easier when they possess mature rules, rewards, standards, memory and reusable capability. Collaboration often dissipates because these supports are weaker.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Competition ≠ Evil
- Collaboration ≠ Good
- Participation ≠ Cumulative Collaboration
- Open Source ⊂ Open Good

## Best worked example
Open-source/scientific collaboration that becomes reusable versus ad hoc collaboration that repeatedly restarts.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
Collaboration can enable free riding, collusion or capture; competition can support discovery and discipline.

## kOA role
Open-good architecture and seeded content as possible cold-start responses. Narrative can make a new regime imaginable before full adoption.

## Epistemic status
Regime framing is interpretive; adoption/network effects are empirical hypotheses.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Who counts as relevant expert and how power follows recognition.

## Exit condition
If collaboration depends on finding relevant competence, how should expertise be recognized without reproducing status?
