# Chapter 7 Brief — Expertise, Credibility and Epistemic Mobility

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Collaborative systems need to know whose contribution is relevant to which question.

## Proof obligation
Separate expertise, credibility, status and authority; define competence as contextual and preserve epistemic mobility.

## Main mechanism
Evidence of qualification/performance supports task-specific competence readings; recognition changes opportunity, so exploration and revisability are necessary.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Equal Dignity ≠ Equal Expertise
- Expertise ≠ Credibility ≠ Status ≠ Authority
- Competence ≠ Mandate
- Unknown ≠ Low Competence
- Confidence ≠ Accuracy

## Best worked example
Unknown practitioner repeatedly performs well despite weak conventional credentials.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
Alternative evidence systems can become new castes or be gamed.

## kOA role
EkoH as contextual reading; portable evidence/attestations. Newcomer-before-expert mechanism is experimental only.

## Epistemic status
Contextual competence is canonical; specific weightings are implementation hypotheses.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Capture/domination, Smart Vote aggregation and attention routing.

## Exit condition
Whoever classifies expertise and credibility acquires power. How is that power constrained?
