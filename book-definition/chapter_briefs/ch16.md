# Chapter 16 Brief — From Decision to Execution

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Chapter 15 ends at legitimate decision, not real-world effect.

## Proof obligation
Explain the execution gap: decisions require ownership, resources, dependencies, sequencing, monitoring, adaptation and closure.

## Main mechanism
Decision becomes executable commitments; commitments become coordinated tasks; feedback reveals blockers/outcomes and triggers adaptation.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Decision ≠ Execution
- Assignment ≠ Capability
- Activity ≠ Outcome
- Escalation ≠ Punishment
- Plan ≠ Reality

## Best worked example
Policy or organizational decision with no owner versus one with explicit operational responsibility.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
More process can become bureaucracy; execution systems can centralize control or optimize the wrong goal.

## kOA role
Orgo as one decision→execution architecture; external institutions can execute through their own systems.

## Epistemic status
Operational mechanisms are implementation patterns; performance must be measured against outcomes.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Replication/federation and cumulative learning.

## Exit condition
If a local architecture can act, how can it spread without recreating a mandatory center?
