# Chapter 18 Brief — Divergence, Forks and Evolution

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Independent instances can change without central permission.

## Proof obligation
Define fork as independent continuation from a common state and show how lineage, bridges and recombination can make divergence cumulative.

## Main mechanism
Common ancestor + deltas preserve comparability; bridges restore needed interfaces; successful innovations can move across branches.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Divergence ≠ Failure
- Divergence ≠ Improvement
- Technical Forkability ≠ Social Forkability
- Fork ≠ Complete Separation
- Kristal Fork ≠ Instance Fork
- Canonical ≠ Permanent

## Best worked example
Captured or poor reference branch versus alternative fork that earns credibility and later influences the reference standard.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
Forks can become sectarian fragmentation; social legitimacy is much harder than code copying.

## kOA role
Instance/Kristal forks, lineage, bridges, reference-branch evolution.

## Epistemic status
Cumulative evolution through divergence/recombination is a systemic hypothesis.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Placement across scales and boundary exchange.

## Exit condition
Branches and institutions exist at many scales. Which functions belong where?
