# Chapter 1 Brief — Distributed Intelligence Is Not Collective Agency

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
The introduction has posed the civilizational coordination paradox.

## Proof obligation
Establish that intelligence and wider capability are already distributed but do not automatically become collective agency.

## Main mechanism
Potential must pass through discovery, interpretation, selection, coordination, execution, evaluation and memory. Capacity can be lost at every conversion.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Distributed Intelligence ⊂ Distributed Potential
- Distributed Intelligence ≠ Collective Agency
- Presence ≠ Accessibility
- Credibility ≠ Contextual Relevance
- Knowing What To Do ≠ Being Able To Do It

## Best worked example
Emerging agricultural disease: knowledge, data, practical skill and resources exist in different places but fail to meet.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
Some failures really are caused by missing knowledge or resources. Do not explain every shortage as coordination loss.

## kOA role
One brief final appearance: kOA as a recurring laboratory focused on interfaces among existing capacities.

## Epistemic status
Core conceptual claims. Examples are illustrative; no implementation proof.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Full coherence theory, loss taxonomy, interface theory, EkoH, Kristal, Orgo and SemantiK.

## Exit condition
If potential exists but agency does not, what system property makes distributed capacities composable?
