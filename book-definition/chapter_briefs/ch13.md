# Chapter 13 Brief — Collective Judgment Without Collective Uniformity

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Semantically comparable contributions still do not form one usable collective judgment.

## Proof obligation
Show how multiple explicit readings over a common participation record synthesize without pretending the collective has one voice.

## Main mechanism
Question type determines aggregation structure; distributions, confidence, expertise, affectedness and subgroup differences can be exposed through lenses.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Facts ≠ Readings
- Aggregation ≠ Understanding
- Majority ≠ Truth
- Expert Consensus ≠ Democratic Mandate
- Lens ≠ Truth
- Default Lens ≠ Neutral Lens
- Preference ≠ Prediction

## Best worked example
Same mean, radically different distribution; or baseline vs expert vs affected-stakeholder readings.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
Infinite lenses recreate overload; weighting can smuggle normative power; self-selected participation is not population representation.

## kOA role
Smart Vote multi-lens layer; EkoH contextual competence; ethiKos structured deliberation.

## Epistemic status
No universal aggregation function; lens designs are explicit implementations.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Attention allocation and democratic authority.

## Exit condition
Once multiple good readings exist, which ones receive scarce attention?
