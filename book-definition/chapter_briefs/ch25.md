# Chapter 25 Brief — Falsifiability and Failure

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Chapter 24 makes empirical comparison possible.

## Proof obligation
End the argument by making theory, design principles and concrete architecture genuinely vulnerable to failure.

## Main mechanism
Decompose claims so mechanism, implementation, adoption, governance and theory can fail separately; predefine evidence that narrows or rejects them.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Implementation Failure ≠ Theory Failure
- Adoption Failure ≠ Mechanism Failure
- Feasibility ≠ Superiority
- Architecture Exists ≠ Hypothesis Validated
- Failure ≠ Confirmation

## Best worked example
High adoption with no downstream gain; rapid technical fork with no credible recovery; deterministic system preserving wrong mappings.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
A theory becomes unfalsifiable by moving goalposts or blaming every negative result on implementation.

## kOA role
kOA must be allowed to fail, be replaced or reveal that theory claims were too strong.

## Epistemic status
Falsifiability is an epistemic requirement; specific experiments require empirical design.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Nothing substantive; the conclusion may synthesize but must not add a hidden premise.

## Exit condition
The conclusion can now ask what it means for a civilization to become better at improving its own capacity to learn and coordinate.
