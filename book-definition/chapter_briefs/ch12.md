# Chapter 12 Brief — Semantic Coherence Across Languages and Institutions

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Pluralism fails if actors cannot preserve meaning across languages and ontologies.

## Proof obligation
Define semantic coherence as preserving conceptual identity, relation, context, uncertainty and provenance without forced universal ontology.

## Main mechanism
Meaning is represented structurally; mappings express equivalence, approximation and non-equivalence; each language realizes meaning through its own resources.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Word ≠ Concept
- Translation ≠ Equivalence
- Mapping ≠ Identity
- Semantic Coherence ≠ Universal Ontology
- Ambiguity ≠ Error
- Fluent Text ≠ Faithful Meaning
- Determinism ≠ Correctness

## Best worked example
A legal or medical concept with no exact equivalent across jurisdictions/languages.

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
Structuring meaning can erase nuance; preserve original expression, uncertainty and local ontology.

## kOA role
SenTient for ambiguity, SemantiK for concepts/mappings, SemantiK Architect for deterministic language-specific realization.

## Epistemic status
Semantic chain is architectural; quality must be validated per language/scope.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Aggregation/lenses and deeper runtime determinism.

## Exit condition
Even comparable meanings can express different judgments. How should those judgments be synthesized?
