# Chapter 10 Brief — Portable Knowledge

**Status:** normalized V2 chapter contract. Higher authority than the detailed chapter specification for scope, canonical distinctions and claim status.

## Entry state
Chapter 9 established reusable memory; now make it independent of the original host.

## Proof obligation
Define portable knowledge as knowledge retaining enough content, structure, provenance and context to remain usable elsewhere.

## Main mechanism
Portability requires specification, lineage, versioning, provenance and independent interpretation—not merely export of bytes.

## Canonical distinctions to protect
- Knowledge Artifact ≠ Platform
- Exportability ≠ Portability
- Claim ≠ Source ≠ Validation
- Institutional Validation ≠ Truth
- Canonical ≠ Mandatory
- Portable ≠ Public

## Best worked example
USB/platform-replacement test: can an independent team read, fork, compare and continue the artifact?

## Serious counterexample / failure pressure
Rich portable formats can become complex; portability can enable misuse or fragmentation.

## kOA role
Kristal as portable/forkable/versioned artifact independent from Konnaxion. Open good as stronger functional portability pattern.

## Epistemic status
Portability→substitutability is a hypothesis; practical tests are proxies.

## Reserved concepts / do not pre-solve
Epistemic pluralism, semantic mappings and system forks.

## Exit condition
If anyone can copy and fork knowledge, how do we preserve plurality without treating all claims as equal?
